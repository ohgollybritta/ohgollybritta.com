The Problem

Convenience shouldn't cost
your privacy.

Commercial voice assistants have always sent audio to the cloud for processing — that was the known trade-off from day one. But for a while, some devices offered a middle ground: an opt-out setting that kept certain voice interactions local, never leaving the hardware. That option is gone now. In early 2025, it was quietly removed. The official reason was AI: newer generative features require more processing power than the device itself can provide, so everything goes to the cloud, without exception, and without a way to decline.

The convenience was never the problem. A voice assistant is genuinely useful for the small, repetitive things — timers, music, reminders. But those tasks don't require a server farm. They never did. The cloud dependency isn't a technical necessity for a kitchen timer. It's a data strategy. "Always-on" and "always uploading" are two different things, and they don't have to come as a package.

"Always-on shouldn't mean
always uploading."
What Changed
In early 2025, the last remaining opt-out for local voice processing was removed from major commercial smart speakers. The stated reason was generative AI — new features need the cloud. The effect was that users lost the ability to keep any part of their voice data on-device, with no alternative offered.
The Gap
No mainstream voice assistant gives you timers, reminders, weather, calendar, and an AI chat interface without a persistent cloud pipeline. Sage is built to close that gap — doing all of it locally, except the things that genuinely need the internet.
The Security Layer
A device that lives on your home network and listens for its name should also be able to defend itself. Sage ships with a firewall enabled, monitors for intrusion attempts, and tells you about them out loud.
Features

The things you actually
use an assistant for.

Sage is built around the tasks that make a voice assistant genuinely useful day-to-day — not a smart home hub, not a product catalog. Timers, weather, reminders, calendar, music, and an optional AI conversation mode. All of it processed locally except what has to leave the device by nature.

⏱
Kitchen Timers
Set named or numbered timers by voice. Preset durations for pasta, rice, eggs. Ask what's running, check time remaining, cancel one or cancel all — hands-free while cooking.
🔔
Reminders
Schedule reminders by voice — "in 20 minutes" or "at 5:30 PM." Sage speaks them back at the right time and sends a push notification to your phone via ntfy, even when you're not in the kitchen.
🌤
Weather Reports
Current conditions, tomorrow's forecast, and outfit tips. Ask "What's the weather?" or "What's the weather tomorrow?" and get a spoken summary — no phone required.
📅
Calendar Briefings
Syncs with Google Calendar via iCal. Ask what's on today or request your full schedule. Good morning briefings include the day's events automatically.
🎵
Spotify Playback
Voice-controlled music — ask for an artist, playlist, or album. Pause, skip, or stop by voice. The audio streams from Spotify; the request to start it stays on-device.
🤖
Claude AI Chat
Say "Hey Claude" to open a full conversation powered by Claude Haiku — fast, lightweight, and built for back-and-forth voice use. Ask recipes, questions, anything. Claude hands timer and reminder requests back to Sage automatically. Completely optional; Sage works standalone without it.
🌙
Bedtime Mode
Say "Goodnight" and Sage goes quiet — notifications silenced, LED off. "Good morning" wakes it with a weather and calendar briefing. Auto-sleep and auto-wake schedules adjust for weekdays vs. weekends, with updated logic for a cleaner transition in and out of sleep mode.
🛡
Security Monitoring
UFW firewall runs on boot, SSH-only. Sage monitors auth logs for failed login attempts and sends an ntfy alert — and speaks one aloud — when brute force activity is detected. Ask "Is the firewall running?" or tell it to turn the firewall on or off by voice.
💡
LED Status Ring
Optional WS2812B 24-LED ring gives visual feedback — green at idle, blue while listening and processing, orange pulse while a timer counts down, red flash when an alarm fires. Off in bedtime mode.
🕐
Time & Date
Ask what time it is, what day it is, or today's date. Quick, local, no cloud required.
📊
System Status
Ask "System status" for a spoken report: uptime, CPU temperature, memory usage, disk space, and firewall state. Sage also checks for system and package updates every three days and notifies you when they're available.
🎭
Personality
Sage doesn't repeat the same phrase twice. Ask it to tell you a joke, ask who made it, or ask who's talking — it has a voice profile system that guesses the speaker. Easter eggs included.
Under the Hood

Python on a
Raspberry Pi 4.

Sage runs entirely on a Raspberry Pi 4 in Python. The goal was to build something that lives in the kitchen like a small appliance — always available, never demanding, and completely understandable by anyone who wants to read the code.

Python-only wasn't just a language preference — it's a constraint that keeps the project readable, accessible, and easy to fork. The entire stack, from wake word detection through command parsing, TTS response, and push notification, is written in one language with no compiled dependencies.

Hardware — Raspberry Pi 4 (4GB), running headless as a dedicated assistant
Wake word — openWakeWord with custom-trained hey_sage.onnx and hey_claude.onnx models
Speech-to-text — Vosk for always-on listening; Whisper for command transcription — both run locally
Text-to-speech — Piper TTS with en_US-lessac-medium voice model, processed on-device
Push notifications — ntfy (self-hostable) for phone alerts when you're not in the room
Internet — used selectively: Spotify streaming, weather API, calendar sync, and optional Claude AI chat
Firewall — UFW enabled on boot, SSH only; Sage monitors auth logs and alerts on failed login attempts via voice and ntfy
Hardware

Everything you need
to build one.

Sage runs on off-the-shelf hardware — nothing proprietary, nothing hard to source. Everything below is available on Amazon. Total cost varies, but you're looking at a one-time purchase with no subscriptions attached.

Raspberry Pi 4 (4GB) — CanaKit Basic Kit includes the board, case, power supply, and SD card.
USB Speaker — Jabra Speak 510 (2025 Edition). USB or Bluetooth, built-in mic array with echo cancellation — significantly improves wake word detection over music.
LED Ring — 5050 WS2812B Addressable LED Ring, DC5V. Optional but adds visual status feedback. Connects to the Pi with three jumper wires, no soldering.
Jumper Wires — ELEGOO 120pc Dupont Wire Kit. Three female-to-female wires are all you need for the LED ring. The rest of the pack will come in handy.
Enclosure
Mine lives in a repurposed candle holder canister — the right diameter for the LED ring, and it looks intentional on a kitchen counter. Any small cylindrical container works. No 3D printer required.
Open Source

Built in the open,
live on GitHub.

The whole point of Sage is that you can see exactly what it does and doesn't do with your voice. Closed-source privacy claims are just claims. Open source is a receipt.

The code is free to use, fork, and adapt for personal and non-commercial use under GPL v3. If you have a Raspberry Pi and a reason to distrust your current smart speaker, you can set it up yourself.
