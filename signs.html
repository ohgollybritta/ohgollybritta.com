Public Awareness

Learn the signs.

Trafficking rarely looks like the movies. It hides in ordinary places: hotels, spas, farms, homes and phones. These are the warning signs worth a closer look.

15,085trafficking cases identified by the U.S. National Human Trafficking Hotline in 2025
21,457victims involved in those cases
2,221of those victims were minors, among cases where age was known

These numbers count only the situations someone reported to the hotline. The real scale is larger.

What to look for

Isolation & control

  • Seems cut off from family, friends, community groups or a place of worship.
  • Is often with someone who speaks for them or decides where they go and who they talk to.
  • Appears to have been coached on what to say.
  • Can’t come and go freely, or lives or works behind unusual security.

Physical & emotional

  • Has bruises or injuries in various stages of healing.
  • Shows signs of being denied food, water, sleep or medical care.
  • Is fearful, timid or submissive.
  • Seems disoriented or confused, or has had a sudden, dramatic change in behavior.

Living & working

  • Lives in unsuitable or overcrowded conditions.
  • Has few personal possessions and no stable place to live.

Children & teens

  • Has suddenly stopped going to school.
  • Has gifts, gift cards, money or a phone they can’t explain, or is secretive about who they talk to online.
  • Is involved in commercial sex in any way. Under U.S. federal law, any minor in commercial sex is a trafficking victim.
No single sign proves trafficking, and not every situation shows every sign. Don’t confront a suspected trafficker or let a possible victim know you’re suspicious. Your safety and theirs come first. Report what you saw and let law enforcement investigate.
For moms

Protecting kids & teens

  • Talk early and often. Explain what grooming looks like: flattery, gifts, secrets and slow pressure from someone who seems to “get” them.
  • Make it safe to tell you. Promise they won’t lose their phone or get in trouble for telling you someone made them uncomfortable.
  • Know their apps and games. Predators reach kids on social media, messaging apps and gaming platforms, then push them to private chats or texting.
  • Name the lures. Offers of gift cards, money, rides, food or a place to stay are common ways strangers hook kids.
  • Remind them people pretend. Someone online may lie about their age or who they are, even with real-looking photos.
  • Lock down accounts. Keep profiles private, review followers together and turn off location sharing.
  • Boys are targets too. Financial sextortion, where a stranger demands money over a photo, most often targets teenage boys.
  • If it happens, stay calm. Don’t pay and don’t delete the messages. Block the person, then report to NCMEC’s CyberTipline and local police. NCMEC’s Take It Down can help remove images.
How to report
Immediate danger Call 911

If someone is in danger right now, call 911 first.

National Human Trafficking Hotline 1-888-373-7888

Text HELP or INFO to 233733. TTY: 711. Free, confidential and open 24/7. humantraffickinghotline.org

Report criminal activity 1-866-347-2423

Homeland Security Investigations tip line, open 24/7. For information about a missing child, call 1-800-THE-LOST.
